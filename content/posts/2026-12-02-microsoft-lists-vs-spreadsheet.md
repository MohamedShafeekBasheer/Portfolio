Most organisations have a shared spreadsheet that has become a small system: a request log, an asset register, an issue tracker. Ten people edit it, someone sorts it wrongly, a row disappears, and nobody knows who changed what.

If a spreadsheet is used to **track things**, rather than calculate, it probably wants to be a **Microsoft List**.

## What a list gives you that a spreadsheet doesn't

- **A form for adding items.** People fill in fields instead of typing into the right row. Nobody edits someone else's entry by accident.
- **Views per person.** "My open items", "Overdue", "Assigned to IT". Each person sees what matters to them, from the same data.
- **Version history on every item.** Who changed the status, and when.
- **Proper column types.** Dates are dates, people are people, choices come from a list. No more "N/A", "n/a" and "none".
- **Permissions, alerts and rules.** "Email me when an item is assigned to me" takes a minute to set up.
- **Power Automate and Power Apps.** Approvals, reminders and a mobile app can be added later on the same data.
- **It works on a phone,** in the Lists app or the browser.

## How to move a spreadsheet to a list

1. Open **Microsoft Lists** (from the Microsoft 365 app launcher, or **New › List** in a SharePoint site).
2. Choose **From Excel** and upload your file, or start from a **template** such as *Issue tracker* or *Asset manager*.
3. Check the column types SharePoint suggests. Change text columns to **Choice**, **Date** or **Person** where it makes sense.
4. Create two or three views people need.
5. Share the list, and make the old spreadsheet read-only so everyone moves over.

Create the list in a **SharePoint site** that belongs to the team, rather than in *My lists*, so it doesn't belong to one person who might leave.

## When Excel is still the right tool

Keep it in Excel when the work is about **calculation**: budgets, models, pivot tables, charts over the numbers. Lists are for records that move through a status; Excel is for working out numbers.

If you need both, keep the records in a list and use **Export to Excel** or Power BI for the analysis.
