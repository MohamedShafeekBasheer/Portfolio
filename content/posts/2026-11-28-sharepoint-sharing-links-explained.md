When you click **Share** on a file in SharePoint or OneDrive, there is a small setting at the top of the dialog that most people never open. It decides who the link works for. It's the single most important choice you make when sharing.

## The four kinds of link

**Specific people.** Only the people you type in can open it. If the link is forwarded, it doesn't work for anyone else. This is the safest choice and the one to use by default.

**People in your organisation.** Anyone signed in with a company account who has the link can open it. Convenient for internal documents. The risk: the link gets forwarded inside the company to people who shouldn't see it.

**People with existing access.** This doesn't give anyone new access. It's a way to send a link to someone who can already open the file, for example "have a look at page 3". Use it when you just want to point someone at a file.

**Anyone.** No sign-in needed. Whoever has the link can open it, including everyone it is forwarded to, inside or outside the company. Your admin may have switched this option off.

You can also choose **Can edit** or **Can view**, and for some file types **Can't download**. If you only need someone to read, choose **Can view**.

## A two-second habit

Before you press **Send**, look at the line that says who the link works for. If it says *Anyone*, ask whether the file would be fine on a public website. If not, change it to *Specific people*.

"Anyone" links are the most common way internal files end up outside the company. Nobody means to do it; the link just travels.

## Check what you've already shared

Open the file's **⋯ › Manage access**. You'll see every link and every person who has access, and you can remove any of them. Doing this for a few sensitive folders now and then is worth it.

## For admins

In the **SharePoint admin center › Policies › Sharing** you can:

- set the **default link type**, for example *Specific people* instead of *Anyone*, so the safe option is the one people get without thinking;
- set an **expiry** for Anyone links, for example 30 days;
- switch **Anyone** links off for the whole organisation, or per site.

Changing the default link type is a quick win: people don't need training, they just get the safer link.
